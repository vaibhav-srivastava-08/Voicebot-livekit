Approved Answers — CNG, Hybrid, Electric, and Fuel Questions

Pre-approved answers about CNG, hybrid variants, electric vehicles, mileage, and fuel options.

Q060: Does Maruti offer a strong hybrid car?

Short answer: Yes, Maruti offers strong hybrid on three models: Grand Vitara, Victoris, and Invicto.

Detailed answer: The Grand Vitara Strong Hybrid claims about 27 kilometer per liter — best in the mid-size SUV segment. The Victoris Strong Hybrid claims about 27 kilometer per liter and is the first Arena car with strong hybrid. The Invicto uses a 2 liter strong hybrid claiming about 23 kilometer per liter. Strong hybrid cars can run on electric power alone at low speeds, offering significant fuel savings especially in city driving.

Caveats: Strong hybrid vehicles cost more than smart hybrid variants. The fuel savings over time can offset the premium for high-usage buyers.

Verified facts: The Grand Vitara Strong Hybrid claims about 27 kilometer per liter — best in the mid-size SUV segment; The Victoris Strong Hybrid claims about 27 kilometer per liter and is the first Arena car with strong hybrid; The Invicto uses a 2 liter strong hybrid claiming about 23 kilometer per liter

Q061: What is the difference between Smart Hybrid and Strong Hybrid in Maruti?

Short answer: Smart Hybrid is a mild hybrid that assists the engine. Strong Hybrid can drive on electric power alone at low speeds. Strong Hybrid gives significantly better mileage.

Detailed answer: Smart Hybrid (or mild hybrid) uses a small electric motor to assist the petrol engine for slightly better efficiency and smoother start-stop. It cannot drive on electric power alone. Strong Hybrid uses a larger electric motor and battery that can drive the car on electric power at low speeds — resulting in significantly better fuel efficiency (e.g., 28 kilometer per liter for Grand Vitara vs 21 kilometer per liter for Smart Hybrid version). Strong Hybrid costs more but saves more fuel.

Caveats: Strong Hybrid is only available on Grand Vitara, Victoris, and Invicto. Smart Hybrid is available across most models.

Verified facts: Strong Hybrid uses a larger electric motor and battery that can drive the car on electric power at low speeds — resulting in significantly better fuel efficiency (e.g., 28 kilometer per liter for Grand Vitara vs 21 kilometer per liter for Smart Hybrid version)

Q064: Which Maruti car is available as an EV?

Short answer: The e Vitara is Maruti's first and currently only electric vehicle, launched in February 2026.

Detailed answer: The e Vitara is an electric SUV available with 49 kWh and 61 kWh battery options. The 61 kWh variant claims over 500 km range. It features ADAS, panoramic sunroof, ventilated seats, V2 liter (Vehicle to Load), and AllGrip-e AWD on select variant. A BaaS (Battery as a Service) option allows lower upfront cost with battery subscription.

Caveats: EV charging infrastructure is still developing in India. Home charging is recommended. Range depends on driving conditions.

Q067: Does Maruti offer turbo petrol cars?

Short answer: Yes, the Fronx offers a 1 liter BoosterJet turbo petrol engine option with 100 PS and 6 speed AT with paddle shifters.

Detailed answer: The Fronx is currently the only Maruti car with a turbo petrol engine. The 1 liter BoosterJet turbo produces 100 PS and is paired with either a 5 speed MT or a 6 speed AT with paddle shifters. It offers a sportier driving experience compared to naturally aspirated engines.

Caveats: Turbo variants are priced higher. The turbo engine requires higher-grade fuel for best performance.

Q079: Is CNG good for highway driving?

Short answer: CNG works on highways but you may notice slightly reduced power compared to petrol mode. Also, CNG stations are less common on highways.

Detailed answer: Maruti S-CNG cars run on both petrol and CNG, so you can switch to petrol on highways if needed. CNG does reduce power slightly (typically 10 to 15 percent) compared to petrol mode. Highway CNG station availability has been improving but is still limited on many routes. Plan your route with CNG stations in mind for long trips. The dual-fuel nature means you are never stranded.

Caveats: CNG is ideal for city use. For highway, carry awareness of CNG station locations. You can always switch to petrol.

Q083: Should I buy petrol or CNG?

Short answer: CNG saves significantly on fuel costs for high daily usage. Petrol is more convenient for occasional use and offers full boot space. CNG is ideal for daily commuters driving 50 plus km per day.

Detailed answer: CNG reduces running cost by roughly 50 percent compared to petrol. It is ideal if you drive 50 km or more daily, have CNG stations nearby, and can manage slightly reduced boot space. Petrol is more convenient for those who drive less, need full boot space, or live in areas with limited CNG stations. All Maruti CNG cars are dual-fuel — they run on both petrol and CNG.

Caveats: CNG tank reduces boot space. CNG stations are concentrated in metros and expanding to other cities. Factory-fitted S-CNG comes with warranty.

Q112: Does any Maruti car have diesel?

Short answer: No, Maruti Suzuki currently does not offer diesel variants in any of its passenger car models in India.

Detailed answer: Maruti discontinued diesel engines from its passenger car lineup. Instead, they focus on petrol, CNG, smart hybrid, strong hybrid, and now electric powertrains. If diesel is a must for you, you would need to look at competitors like Hyundai, Tata, Kia, or Mahindra. Maruti's strong hybrid offers comparable or better efficiency than many diesel cars.

Q128: Should I buy petrol, CNG, or hybrid?

Short answer: Petrol is convenient and offers full performance. CNG cuts running costs by about 50 percent for high daily use. Strong hybrid offers premium efficiency without refueling hassle.

Detailed answer: Choose petrol if: you drive moderate distances, want simplicity, and have varied driving needs. Choose CNG if: you drive 50 plus km daily in the city and have CNG stations nearby — the savings are significant. Choose Strong Hybrid if: you want the best efficiency without CNG infrastructure dependency, drive in mixed city-highway conditions, and can afford the premium. Smart Hybrid is a mild upgrade over petrol with slightly better efficiency.

Caveats: CNG reduces boot space. Strong hybrid costs more upfront. Each fuel type has trade-offs.

Q154: Should I buy Maruti or wait for an EV?

Short answer: If you need a car now, Maruti offers excellent options including hybrid for efficiency. If you can wait, the EV landscape is expanding with models like e Vitara.

Detailed answer: The EV market in India is still developing — charging infrastructure, range anxiety, and higher prices are current challenges. Maruti's strong hybrid cars (Grand Vitara, Victoris, Invicto) offer 25 to 28 kilometer per liter, which approaches EV-like efficiency without charging dependency. If you want to go electric now, the e Vitara is available. For most buyers currently, a petrol/CNG/hybrid Maruti remains the practical choice.

Caveats: Technology is evolving rapidly. Buy based on current needs rather than waiting indefinitely.
